# Rewrite dilemmas so both sides bleed equally

The current 223 Pick Your Poison dilemmas fail less on clarity than on tension. By hand audit, about **156 are "playable" but only about 20 to 25 are genuinely strong**, and roughly **36 are lopsided or cost-free, 18 are vague, and 11 are non-parallel**. The fix is a disciplined card template: two sides that start with the same verb form, run 6 to 12 words, spend two different everyday currencies (time vs dignity, pain vs shame, sleep vs money), and put the cost last. Each card must pass a "what would the 30% say?" test, because the minority-defends mechanic needs a sayable, funny defence on both sides. The system (insurer, EHR, schedule, waiting room) is always the villain; patients, conditions, and bodies are never the punchline. Technically a rewrite is low-risk: no code references any dilemma id, the binding rules are 80 characters per side, no em dashes, no "would you rather" prefix, and minimum pools of 100 universal and 55 clinical entries. Published evidence on this niche is thin (no vote-split datasets, no designer write-ups), so most craft rules below are labelled judgment and should be calibrated against logged in-game vote splits.

## Parse in three seconds: parallel grammar, one idea, cost last

The phone screen is the binding constraint. No source measures parse time for two-option text, so the rules here are inference built on two supported ideas. Jackbox writers warn that extra detail is "way too much info to process" and advise one specific, absurd constraint ([Jackbox](https://www.jackboxgames.com/how-to-write-a-good-quiplash-prompt)), and they hold that if the writer cannot answer their own prompt it will not work. The WYR analogue is that the author must be able to argue both sides instantly.

The proposed rules: each side is one idea (one noun phrase or verb phrase with at most one modifier that does real work); both sides open with the same verb form ("Always...", "Lose...") so the eye only diffs the ending; each side runs **6 to 12 words with a hard cap near 14**; and the cost goes last because it is what the reader hunts for. There is no setup line, since the two sides are the whole card. Avoid double-barrelled sides unless they are a deliberate one-benefit-plus-one-cost pairing, avoid negation stacks ("not have to"), and keep both sides within about one line of each other, because length asymmetry reads as detail asymmetry and biases votes toward the more vivid side.

The audit confirms these are the actual failure signatures. Non-parallel entries such as wyr-006 ("see everything your doctor types about you, live" vs "never have to watch your doctor type again") make B the absence of A. Entries with a 20+ character imbalance between sides (wyr-067, 132, 149, 225, 235, p002, p010, p022, p023, p024, and others) are consistently the ones with a joke bolted onto one side. Muddled wording (wyr-172, p024, p036, 232, 246) fails when the UI reads the card aloud as "A. Or. B." The game controller literally speaks `{a}. Or. {b}.`, so entries must be lowercase-initial fragments completing "Would you rather...", with no terminal punctuation. Existing sides average 47 to 48 characters with a longest of 68, so the rewrite can tighten to roughly 60 without friction.

The best existing cards are the template to copy. wyr-206 (doctor 90 minutes late but gives you an hour vs on time and gives you seven minutes) and wyr-234 (warm hands and cold personality vs cold hands and warm personality) share the same anatomy: plain words, 35 to 60 characters, one clause per side, a real cost on each, and costs in different currencies. Five reusable templates emerge from the audit: intensity vs duration or frequency (wyr-109, 165, 203), competence vs warmth (wyr-012), mirror roles (p011, p027, p052), audience swap (mom vs boss, boss vs ex: wyr-211, 213), and information vs delay (wyr-201, p005).

## Lopsided cards share seven causes, each with a cheap fix

Public WYR guides agree that sides must be "comparable in weight" with offsetting advantages and drawbacks, and that a clear winner kills the debate; the stock no-brainer is a bug-eating dare vs $1,000 ([Science of People](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/would-you-rather-questions/)). The audit shows the existing pool fails in exactly these ways, and every failure maps to a repeatable fix.

| Cause | Audit example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Free perk vs free perk, no cost | wyr-138 (cast signed by everyone vs cast plays music), 143, 174, 249, p059 | Give each perk an unavoidable recurring cost in a different currency |
| Dominated option | wyr-159 (never catch a cold, sneeze hourly vs catch every cold, never sneeze), 177, 252, 258, p056 | Add a hidden upside to the loser or a cost to the winner |
| Joke side vs mundane side | wyr-129 (vet treats you like a golden retriever vs doctor like a tax audit), 222, 259 | Make both sides equally concrete, or put the joke in the plain side's cost |
| Magnitude mismatch | wyr-181 (bed folds you in half vs creaks), wyr-029 (naps only inside a running MRI) | Keep both within one order of "how much I'd complain" |
| Loophole | wyr-033 (rooster at 4:00 vs page at 4:07) | Name the constraint so "I'd sleep through it" disappears |
| Be-an-organ whimsy with no stakes | wyr-070 (be the liver vs be a kidney), 147, 176, 067, 066 | Cut, or convert to a stakes-bearing role trade |
| History-trivia gifts | wyr-097, 123, p038, p039 | Cut, since the player gets a free moral win |

Loss aversion adds a framing rule. Tversky and Kahneman estimated that gains must be about twice equal losses to feel balanced, though replication is mixed for small stakes ([JDM 2012](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/11/11303a/jdm11303a.html)). The practical consequence is that a "lose X" side outweighs a "gain X" side of equal size, so frame both sides as costs (avoidance-avoidance) or both as gains, never mixed. Two further patterns hurt the pool as a whole. First, repeated premises (sneeze/hiccup/cold appears in about ten entries, dentist in seven, "doctor who..." in over 25) make a single eight-card game feel samey, and the existing dedup test only catches exact a+b matches, so near-duplicates need a human pass. Second, several Pro entries are pasted from Normal templates (p006 reuses wyr-240, p020 reuses wyr-137). A spelling cleanup is also due: "apologising", "neighbour", and "recognises" appear in an otherwise US pool and no test catches them.

## Agony comes from different currencies and values conflicts, not bigger numbers

Two-bad-options cards are the right default. Lewin's taxonomy distinguishes approach-approach, avoidance-avoidance, and approach-avoidance conflicts ([Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Approach-avoidance_conflict)), and avoidance-avoidance decisions are classically slower and harder to resolve. One study found that advantage holds only in a positive affective context ([HUJI](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-relative-difficulty-of-resolving-motivational-conflicts-is-af/)), which is what a light party mood supplies. Computational work also finds that approach and avoidance drives of near-equal strength produce the most hesitant responses ([PMC10023482](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10023482)), which supports the equal-pull target. Do not lean on "more options cause paralysis": a 40-replication project (n = 26,703) cast doubt on the choice-deferral effect ([Evangelidis, Levav, Simonson 2023](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4484)), and WYR is forced choice anyway.

The levers are therefore design choices. **Different currencies** (time vs dignity, pain vs shame, self vs others) stop a card collapsing into arithmetic; a card spending one currency on both sides ("lose $500 vs lose $600") is fragile. **Recurring vs one-time** cost (one unforgettable disaster vs a mild annoyance forever) splits on dread tolerance. **Certainty vs gamble** splits on risk temperament, which defends well because it is a personality axis, not a right answer. **Values conflicts** (honesty vs kindness, autonomy vs safety) divide rooms along real moral lines: the trolley data show sacrifice endorsement of 81% (Switch), 72% (Loop), and 51% (Footbridge) across 70,000 responses ([Daily Nous](https://dailynous.com/?p=26753)), and Footbridge is the textbook near-50/50. Medical life supplies these values conflicts directly. In a 5,030-patient study, time with the physician predicted satisfaction more strongly than wait length ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1810532/)), so "seen in ten minutes by someone skimming your chart vs a 90-minute wait for a doctor who listens" is a true two-sided trade. Patients accept 3 to 4 week waits for their own PCP on sensitive or chronic concerns (68.2% and 61.1%) but only 7.2% on urgent issues ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/patients-are-willing-wait-to-see-their-own-doctor)), so the same continuity-vs-access pair flips with one stakes variable and yields two cards.

## Balance tests and the minority-defends mechanic set the real bar

The mechanic changes the target. Pick Your Poison currently pays a flat 100 for the majority side, and project research recommended graded split prediction and a visible but sheddable minority marker. Split the Room scores even splits explicitly ([Jackbox](https://www.jackboxgames.com/split-the-room)). A perfect 50/50 is unpredictable, so graded prediction works best on cards that land roughly **35/65 to 45/55**; a 20/80 card is acceptable only if the minority has a funny defence. Treat the 35/65 figure as a working threshold, since no dataset of real split distributions exists. The only vote data found is incidental (a BuzzFeed relationship poll at 53/47, [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/benhenry/from-disney-to-dick-and-everything-between)).

Run six tests on every card before it ships. The swap test reorders the sides and swaps who suffers; a verdict that flips signals position bias. The "30% sentence" test requires the writer to produce, within ten seconds, a believable one-line defence of the minority side; if it is forced, the card is lopsided. A cost-symmetry check lists the currencies each side spends and prefers different ones. A defender-hook check confirms each side has a principle, a secretly loved loophole, or an absurd upside to argue; the audit shows why, since entries like wyr-070 offer nothing for the Against Medical Advice defence round. A three-reader check sorts cards: 3-0 gets rewritten, 2-1 ships, and visible hesitation beyond five seconds is a good sign. Finally, the live ship rule is to log the per-card split in-game and cull cards outside roughly 35/65 after 20 or more votes. Avoid pure taste splits (pineapple pizza) that are predictable by demographics, and avoid sides that are verdicts on a person present, such as weight, sexuality, or ability, so minority stigma lands on opinions about situations and not on bodies.

## Medical angles: lay pain for Normal, workflow trade-offs for Pro

The sharpest shared experiences are system-as-antagonist: insurance, administrators, charting, pagers, waiting, gowns, and TV-medicine inaccuracy. Dr. Glaucomflecken casts a reasonable person against an absurd system in about 90 seconds, with insurers and administrators as villains and patients never the target ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2022/03/25/dr-glaucomflecken-will-flanary-profile/)). Gomerblog's pattern is a bureaucratic logic taken one step too far, delivered deadpan ([Gomerblog](https://gomerblog.com/tag/hospital-administrators/)), and it converts naturally to dilemmas: offer two administrator policies and make the player pick the lesser absurdity. Adam Kay's book, with over two million copies sold, shows the wry-system register crosses to lay readers ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/this-is-going-to-hurt-by-adam-kay-1.3537331)). Existing commercial nurse WYR packs lean on night vs day and slow vs busy shifts ([Nursa](https://community.nursa.com/forums/topic/2530-would-you-rather/)), which is the genre baseline to beat: safe, but mostly "pick the lesser annoyance."

For the general (Normal) audience, the research recommends plain-language twins of insider objects: prior auth becomes "your insurer overruling your doctor," a handoff becomes "explaining everything to a stranger at shift change," and "q4h vitals" becomes "being woken at 4 a.m. to be told to rest." Strong lay seeds pair currencies: speed vs being heard, continuity vs soonest slot, telehealth with a frozen doctor vs a receptionist reading your reason for visiting aloud, a $240 prescription today vs $20 in three days, a itemized $12 tissue line vs a single "services" line, being woken every four hours for a number vs left alone all night. The flossing lie is a safe universal confession currency, with about 27% of US adults admitting to lying to their dentist ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2015/06/24/417184367/are-you-flossing-or-just-lying-about-flossing-the-dentist-knows)), but a bare honest-vs-lie card is a no-brainer unless the lie has a price the player cares about.

For staff (Pro), the trades are ones staff already make: sleep vs pay, autonomy vs backup, bedside presence vs charting, short call blocks vs fewer handoffs. Documentation data is directional but strong: a KLAS survey of 80,147 acute care nurses found 79% lose time to unproductive charting and 34% spend three or more hours a week on duplicative documentation ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/79-of-nurses-lose-time-to-unproductive-charting-klas/)). The FIRST duty-hours trial showed flexible shift rules improved continuity and satisfaction while shorter shifts raise handoffs ([ABS](https://www.absurgery.org/resources/news/initial-first-trial-results-announced/)), so rest vs continuity has no settled winner, which is exactly the property a dilemma needs. Resident views on the 80-hour cap split 23% appropriate, 41% too many, 34% not enough in one orthopedic survey ([JAOA](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.7556/jaoa.2010.110.7.385/html?lang=en)). Call structures differ in kind, not just amount (short frequent call vs home call vs rare brutal call: [UCLA](https://residency.semel.ucla.edu/call/), [UF](https://urology.ufl.edu/educational/welcome-urology-resident-applicants/resident-on-call-schedule)), which supports "frequent light vs rare brutal" pairs. The research produced 60 seed tension pairs (28 public, 32 staff); the highest-value are cross-audience mirrors, such as charting-vs-bedside (staff) beside typing-vs-looking-up (patient) as a "both sides of the desk" round. Specialty stereotypes work in Pro only when both options are self-targeted or flattering-ish. One caution: no real Reddit threads (r/nursing, r/residency, r/medicine) were read, so the staff-pain claims rest on surveys and official pages and need a staff playtester's check, especially seeds on pay vs sleep, autonomy vs support, and locum vs staff, which are general-knowledge inference.

## Safety boundaries keep the system as the only villain

The boundary reuses the project's established rule: the butt of the joke is a system, an object, the player's own ego, or a specialty, never patients as a class, conditions, disability, weight, addiction, mental illness, age, religion, or culture. The evidence for caution is specific. In a survey of 710 NZ mental health staff, 79.4% found black humour beneficial, but they saw harm when it trivializes distress or comes at a patient's expense, and benefit was lower among Maori/Pasifika staff and non-native English speakers ([PMC10809729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809729/)). Medical students have called patients seen as obese or with "self-inflicted" conditions "fair game" (Wear et al. 2006, cited in the project's humor research), which is the pattern to avoid. Do not use the word "gomer" in the game.

The derived dilemma rules are concrete. No option may involve a real patient being harmed or dying or a named condition as the punchline; severe outcomes appear only in distant or historical framing, and the stronger recommendation is to remove mortality entirely and use time, money, dignity, sleep, and control as currencies. Both options should land on the same victim, the player. "Difficult patient" setups flip to clinician helplessness against the system. Gross-out is acceptable for the player's own body in a low-stakes frame but not for identity-linked conditions (incontinence in the elderly, STIs, weight, mental health, addiction, fertility, pregnancy loss). Name no real hospitals, EHR brands, pharma companies, or brand drugs (generic drug and condition names are free), and nothing may read as treatment guidance, so "skip your antibiotics" cards are out. Normal must pass a waiting-room read-aloud test; Pro must pass a break-room-with-family-walking-by test. Lay reaction to clinician humor and the effect on currently ill players are untested, so the mitigation is to avoid illness identity entirely.

## Technical constraints make the rewrite low-risk but demand one-for-one replacement

The content lives in two files: `dilemmas` (135 Normal, all `breadth: 'universal'`) in `packages/content/src/packs/dilemmas.ts` and `dilemmasPro` (88 clinical) in `dilemmas-pro.ts`, merged in `packages/content/src/index.ts`. Each entry is a single line `{ id: 'wyr-NNN', a: '...', b: '...', breadth: 'universal' },` using single-quoted TS with curly quotes inside. Tests in `content.test.ts` enforce unique ids, unique a+b text (exact match only), at least 100 universal and 55 clinical, no em dashes anywhere, at least 100 total, `a` not starting with "would you rather", and `a.length <= 80` and `b.length <= 80`. The prefix test only checks `a`, but both sides should comply. No code, test, or doc references a specific dilemma id, so ids can be renumbered freely and the 28 Normal-numbered ids "moved" into the Pro file can be folded into `wyr-pNNN`.

Delivery details shape the copy. The game draws 8 dilemmas per game; the last is "final" and worth triple. Normal draws 8 universal; Pro draws 6 clinical plus 2 universal (`PRO_SHARE = 0.7`), so universal quality matters in both modes. Pools recycle per room, so 100 Normal supports about 12 games before repeats and 55 Pro about 9 games of clinical draws. That argues for **120+ universal and 70+ clinical** after the rewrite. Cutting about 50 weak Normal entries without replacement would drop the pool to 85 and fail the 100-minimum test, so replace one-for-one. Because the dedup test is weak, authors must police near-duplicate premises by hand. No pick-rate telemetry exists, so every "lopsided" label in this report is editorial judgment; the Pro "good" tally (65) was hand-counted and should be recounted by script before quoting. The 223 total was verified by script.

## Conclusion

The rewrite problem is a currency problem more than a wording problem. Existing "good" cards are readable but cheap, because most spend one mild currency or give a free perk to one side; the strong ones (wyr-012, 206, 234, p058) win by pitting two different things players value against each other in plain words. The practical implication is a short authoring loop: pick a tension from the seed catalog, assign each side a different currency, write both sides with the same verb frame and the cost last, then run the swap and 30%-sentence tests before the card enters the pool.

The larger unknown is empirical. No source offers real WYR split distributions, parse-time data for phone text, or Jackbox's own vetting method, so the 35/65 cull band, the 6 to 12 word target, and the whole test battery are untested craft. The most valuable next step is not more writing but instrumentation: log the per-card pick split and defence outcomes in-game, use the first few hundred rounds to cull and re-tune, and treat the rewrite as a first calibrated draft. Before mass-writing, follow the project's sampling rule: write a short batch in the deadpan, no-em-dash house voice and approve it first.
