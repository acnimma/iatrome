# Visual and Motion Design Language for Jackbox-Quality Party Games (Stage + Phone)

Scope note: Jackbox has published very little first-party material on typography, layout, or reveal choreography. Their blog and interviews talk about principles (clarity, hierarchy, pacing, one task at a time) and not specs. I could not get past the Jackbox Fandom wiki (HTTP 402) or gameuidatabase.com (HTTP 403), and searches found no GDC talk on Jackbox UI. Anything below about how specific screens look comes from my own memory of playing the games. It is labelled "unverified recollection" and kept in Inferences or Gaps, never in Cited Findings. The web-platform guidance (fluid type, animation performance, reduced motion, TV safe areas) is well sourced.

Codebase context (local observation from the iatrome repo, 2026-11-20, not a web source): `apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css` defines palette, radii, shadows, `--play-raised` text shadow and two easings (`--ease-out`, `--ease-spring`). It has no type scale or space scale tokens. Components use one-off `clamp()` values with vw middles, for example `clamp(2.4rem, 5.5vw, 4.6rem)` in RoomBits, `clamp(2.6rem, 6vw, 4.6rem)` in Podium and `clamp(16px, 2.6vw, 28px)` gaps in page.module.css. Answers and prompts use `--font-play` (Dogbangy) with a raised text shadow. This explains much of the "inconsistent spacing / not cohesive" complaint: every component picks its own curve.

## 1. How Quiplash 2/3, Drawful 2, Trivia Murder Party 2, Fibbage and Survey Scramble present prompts, answers, votes and winners on the big screen

### Takeaway
Jackbox's published principles: a clear hierarchy where nothing competes for attention, art that serves the mechanic, TV-show pacing with one thing happening at a time, and spotlighting the players' content rather than the game's. Reveals are sequential and suspenseful. In Fibbage, lies are revealed one at a time with who fell for them, and the truth comes last. In Quiplash, two answers go head to head, votes are shown, and a unanimous win gets a special "Quiplash" callout. I found no first-party sources for exact typography or animation timing.

### Cited Findings
- Jackbox art leads say the art should be "striking enough to make the game stand out visually, without distracting from the gameplay." It should also support accessibility, because visual cues guide players and affect how approachable a game is for disabled and inexperienced players. — [Jackbox blog: Behind the scenes of PP10 art](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- Bruno Rodríguez (Fixytext) on hierarchy: is the hierarchy of elements clear, or is "everything [is] competing for attention and confusing the players?" — [Jackbox blog: PP10 art](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- Kyle Fleischer: "I try to tie the visuals of a Party Pack game to its mechanics," so players understand what they are doing and why. Stef Egan: backdrops should be "visually engaging without being overly detailed or distracting from the characters and action." — [Jackbox blog: PP10 art](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- Bruno Rodríguez: the games share structure and general UI, and "the art sets them apart". So in a pack, the shared UI system gives cohesion and the per-game art gives identity. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 art](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- PP10 directors: Tim Sniffen says the director's job is to make the player experience "the best and clearest it can be". Warren Arnold says it means "making sure all parts of the game work as a cohesive unit". Sniffen also recalls a Tee K.O. 2 bug where "the game played EVERY SINGLE ANIMATION that was possible at the same time" (a cautionary example of animation overload). — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)
- Survey Scramble's director (Sniffen): "We never want to outshine the people actually playing, and this one even more so." — [Digital Trends: Survey Scramble interview](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/the-jacbox-survey-scramble-interview/)
- The Jack Principles, written by founder Harry Gottlieb, centre on an "Interactive Conversation Interface" in which a live host seems to guide players. The principles are to maintain pacing (limit choices, one task at a time), create the illusion of awareness, and maintain it. Allard Laban (CCO) says the games borrow TV conventions such as narrative timing, transitions and dialogue, creating a "weird hybrid of passive and active media." — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- Laban: most of the target emotions are about laughing and making friends laugh, and winning is secondary. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- Quiplash structure: each prompt is seen by two random players. The prompt and the two answers are shown together, the other players and the audience vote for the funnier one, and authors score by votes received. An answer that wins every other player's vote earns a "quiplash" and bonus points. Identical answers produce a "jinx" and no points. The final round ("The Last Lash" in Quiplash 1) has everyone answer one shared prompt. — [Wikipedia: Quiplash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiplash)
- Quiplash 3's final round is "Thriplash", where prompts ask for three responses. Quiplash 3 also adds moderation so responses can be checked before they appear on screen. — [Jackbox: Quiplash 3](https://www.jackboxgames.com/quiplash-three); [What Pack Is That (fan catalogue)](https://swagsteve.github.io/What-Pack-Is-That/game-pages/pack7/quiplash3.html)
- Fibbage reveal order: players see all answers (their lies, sometimes house lies, and the truth). After voting, the game runs through the lies that players picked, showing who picked each. It reveals the truth last, with the players who found it. The end screen names the winner and the players whose lies got the most "props". (Several reviews and retail pages; one third-party site gives the point values as 1000 for the truth and 500 per player fooled, doubling and tripling in later rounds. Unconfirmed by Jackbox.) — [Fibbage review summaries via search: TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/fibbage-review), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/fibbage-xbox-one-review), [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/fibbage-review-your-next-hit-party-game/)
- Survey Scramble's modes (Hilo, Speed, Bounce, Squares) reveal answers on a ranked board or list, Family-Feud style. Hilo is "more casual in its pace" and Speed is "a much more hectic version". The interview does not describe the reveal animations. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/the-jacbox-survey-scramble-interview/)
- Spencer Ham (Timejinx) says the score "alternating between your score growing and shrinking felt cool and novel". Score movement is treated as a designed moment. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)

### Inferences
- **Hierarchy rule for every stage screen:** one hero element at a time. During answering it is the prompt. During a matchup it is the two answers, with the prompt demoted to a smaller header. During the reveal it is the vote tally and then the winner. During scoring it is the leaderboard. Everything else (room code, timer, host controls) is shrunk, desaturated or hidden. This applies the "nothing competing for attention" principle above.
- **Players' text is the star.** Since "we never want to outshine the people", answer cards should be the cleanest, highest-contrast, largest-type objects on screen. Decoration goes on the frame (jelly gloss, outline, shadow), never on the letterforms of long answers.
- **Unverified recollection of Quiplash 2/3 matchup screens:** the prompt sits at the top in a large heavy sans, sentence case, inside a banner. The two answers sit side by side in big rounded boxes, either speech-bubble-like or tablet-like, with roughly equal weight. When voting ends, voter avatars or names stack under the answer each one chose, then a percentage or point total appears and the author's name is revealed. The game does not attribute the answers while voting. A unanimous win triggers a large "QUIPLASH!" stamp-style callout over the winning card. Quiplash 3 is remembered as having a tactile 3D, clay-like look. I could not find a source to confirm any of these details. The anonymity during voting, followed by attribution at the reveal, is the most important pattern to copy, since it creates the "who wrote that?!" laugh.
- **Unverified recollection of Fibbage, Drawful 2 and Trivia Murder Party 2:** Fibbage and Drawful show the 4 to 8 answer options as a grid of uniform tiles or pills. Reveals then go one tile at a time: the tile pops forward, the people who picked it slide in, the author is revealed, and points are shown. The truth gets a distinct colour and a bigger hold. TMP2 uses a horror-host frame, but its question and answer typography is still plain, high-contrast and heavy. These are recollections only.
- **Case and type:** given Dogbangy is chunky and rounded, set prompts and answers in sentence case, not all caps. All caps reduces word-shape recognition and makes long strings much wider. Reserve all caps or Dogbangy-only treatment for short callouts ("QUIPLASH"-equivalent, "JINX", round titles, room code). This is a typographic inference and was not confirmed for Jackbox titles.
- **A recommended type split for iatrome:** Dogbangy for prompts up to about 90 characters, answers up to about 60 characters, names, scores and callouts. Inter (bold or semibold, large) for long case stems and any answer over the threshold. That keeps the bubbly voice where it is legible and avoids chunky-font fatigue on long text. The existing `--font-play` comment already states this fallback intent ("Long case stems fall back to the body face"), so it needs to be enforced by length.
- **Why the current look reads as "flat":** a `text-shadow` stack on type (`--play-raised`) gives depth to letters, but if the card behind is a flat colour without its own depth (gloss, outline, drop shadow, slight tilt) the composition still reads flat. Jackbox-style polish puts depth on the containers: framed answer cards, stamps and banners that sit above a textured or vignetted background. Text stays crisp and solid-coloured on top.

Recommendation: the report writer should flag that screenshots and teardown videos would need manual review.

## 2. Phone controller UX patterns (layout, button sizing, voting screens, long text, waiting states, submit feedback)

### Takeaway
Jackbox deliberately keeps the controller to a tiny set of components: draw, pick from a set of buttons, or enter text. It asks for exactly one action per moment, so once players learn a few games they know every controller. It must run on old phones in a plain browser with no app. Accessibility settings that touch the controller and pacing include extended timers, no timers, a motion-sensitivity toggle, subtitles, alt text viewable on the controller, and "start game from controller only".

### Cited Findings
- Chase McClure (senior software engineer with a UX background, hired in 2019 as the phone's "advocate"): "We try to keep it pretty simple." The controller's components are drawing, choosing between a collection of buttons, or entering text, so players who learn a few games know the basic controls for all of them. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- Laban: designers never ask for multiple choices at once, and each moment asks for one action. He also said "if he finds an interface hard, my aunt's going to find it very difficult." — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- The phone allows private, role-specific information (for example Push the Button shows different questions to different roles), which a shared console controller can't. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- Jackbox aims to work on nearly any phone with an internet connection, not only the newest devices, and joining requires no app download. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- PP7 added a motion-sensitivity toggle in Quiplash 3, Champ'd Up and Talking Points that "reduces motion in games with many moving parts". It also added subtitles, separate volume sliders (music, SFX, host VO), room codes read aloud, and alt text for Talking Points images that players can view from the browser controller. — [Jackbox: PP7 accessibility](https://www.jackboxgames.com/new-accessibility-features-in-the-jackbox-party-pack-7/)
- PP10 has motion sensitivity, subtitles, Extended Timers, No Timers, "Start Game from Controller Only" and content filters. — [Jackbox: PP10 accessibility](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/accessibility-features-in-the-jackbox-party-pack-10)
- Jackbox support states that controller accessibility issues are "probably a bug", meaning the controller is meant to be accessible by default. — [Jackbox support: accessibility features](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794801592855-What-accessibility-features-are-available-in-your-games)
- FixyText needed "a form of internal clock to sync up any text changes" across phones, to avoid "a garbled mess of letters". In other words, state sync is part of legibility. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)

### Inferences
- **The iatrome controller primitive set** should be locked to: (a) a single prompt and text field with a big submit; (b) a vertical list of full-width vote buttons; (c) a waiting/feedback card; (d) the draw pad. Every game mode reuses these four, matching "learn once, know all".
- **Voting screen pattern:** full-width stacked buttons, one per option, in reading order matching the stage. That means the left card on stage is button 1 on the phone, and a shared letter or colour chip (the existing `.letter` bubble in `answers.module.css`) ties phone button to stage card. Minimum touch target is 48 to 56 px tall. For long answers, let buttons grow vertically and wrap. Never truncate an answer someone is voting on. Hide the player's own answer or show it disabled with a "yours" label (unverified recollection: Jackbox does not let you vote for your own answer).
- **Long text on phone:** use Inter for vote-button text above about 40 characters. Use `text-wrap: pretty` for multi-line answers and `text-wrap: balance` for the prompt header (see section 3). Keep the prompt pinned at the top in a smaller size, with options scrolling beneath if needed.
- **Submit feedback:** an immediate, local, optimistic state change within 100 ms (the button squishes, then the screen swaps to a "Sent" card with the submitted text echoed back). Follow with a waiting state that shows something to look at: who is still writing, as avatars greying in. That keeps the "illusion of awareness" from the Jack Principles alive while waiting.
- **Waiting states should say what is happening on the TV** ("Look up! Votes are in"), because during reveals the phone should go quiet so eyes go to the stage. This follows the one-focus hierarchy rule.
- **Timers:** respect a host-level "extended timers / no timers" setting, as Jackbox does. On phone, the timer ring is secondary to the input.

### Gaps
- No published Jackbox spec on button sizes, controller fonts, or exact voting-screen layouts. The guidance above combines general mobile touch-target practice with unverified recollection.
- I found no source on how Jackbox truncates or scales long player answers on the controller.

## 3. Responsive and scaling strategy for a stage on a 720p TV, 1440x900 laptop, ultrawide, or phone-only mode

### Takeaway
Use a token-driven fluid type and space scale (Utopia method: interpolate between a small-screen and a large-screen modular scale using `clamp()` with a rem plus viewport or container term). Constrain the stage to a centered 16:9-ish safe frame with about 5% margins, size answer-card text with container query units so cards scale with their slot rather than the viewport, and use `text-wrap: balance` for prompts. Keep max/min type ratios at or below 2.5 for zoom accessibility.

### Cited Findings
- Utopia's method: define a type scale at a minimum viewport and at a maximum viewport (base size and ratio at each), and let the browser interpolate between them. The space scale derives from type step 0 using multipliers. — [Smashing Magazine: Meet Utopia](https://smashingmagazine.com/2021/04/designing-developing-fluid-type-space-scales/)
- Utopia precalculates `clamp(min, intercept + slope*100vw, max)` (Pedro Rodriguez's formula). Example: 1rem to 2rem between 320px and 1440px gives `clamp(1rem, 0.7143rem + 1.4286vw, 2rem)`. Changing any bound requires recalculation. Utopia warns that clamp can cap the maximum size when users zoom text, citing Adrian Roselli, and says to "ensure you test thoroughly". — [Utopia blog: clamp](https://utopia.fyi/blog/clamp/)
- Utopia's output is CSS custom properties `--step--2 … --step-5`, each a `clamp()`. utopia-core can output `cqi` instead of `vi`, and it flags steps that fail WCAG SC 1.4.4 (200% zoom). — [npm utopia-core](https://npmjs.com/utopia-core); [Utopia fluid scales skill listing](https://smithery.ai/skills/matthewharwood/utopia-fluid-scales)
- Container query units: set `container-type: inline-size` on the wrapper and size text in `cqi`. Text sized only by viewport or container ignores user zoom, so add a rem term, for example `clamp(1rem, 0.875rem + 0.5cqi, 1.25rem)`. Chrome guidance says max should not exceed 2.5 times min for accessibility. Units are supported since Chromium 105, Safari 16 and Firefox 110. Container units can't measure the element they're set on (they need an ancestor container), and on `html` they fall back to the viewport. — [web.dev: fluid type with Baseline features](https://web.dev/articles/baseline-in-action-fluid-type); [Chrome: Fluid scaling guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/css/fluid-scaling); [web.dev: container queries in action](https://web.dev/articles/baseline-in-action-container-queries)
- Fit-to-width text (scale a short string to exactly fill its box) can be done in pure CSS with registered custom properties and `tan(atan2())` over container units (Roman Komarov), or with a small script that sets the size from character count (Andy Bell's riff). — [kizu.dev: Fit-to-width text](https://kizu.dev/fit-to-width/); [Piccalilli: riffing on fit text](https://piccalil.li/blog/riffing-on-the-latest-css-fit-text-approach/)
- `text-wrap: balance` evens line lengths for short text such as headings. It only applies to blocks of 6 or fewer lines in Chromium and 10 or fewer in Firefox. `text-wrap: pretty` reduces orphans on longer text at some performance cost. It has been Baseline widely available since March 2024. — [MDN: text-wrap](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/text-wrap)
- TV guidance: TVs are viewed from about 10 ft. Keep must-see UI inside about a 5% overscan-safe margin. Android uses 48dp sides and 27dp top/bottom in one doc and 58/28dp in another, and an older doc says 10%. Fire TV says keep text within the inner 90%. Roku says at least a 5% margin. Android suggests designing for 1080p (960x540 dp base) and letting it downscale to 720p. — [Android: Design for TV](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/foundations/design-for-tv); [Android TV layouts](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/layouts); [Amazon Fire TV UX guidelines](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/fire-tv/design-and-user-experience-guidelines.html); [Roku key design principles](https://developer.roku.com/dev/docs/key-design-principles)

### Inferences
- **Stage frame:** render the stage into a centered frame, `width: min(100vw, 177.78vh)` and `aspect-ratio: 16/9`, with `container-type: size` and `container-name: stage`. Pad it with a 5% safe inset (`padding: 2.8cqh 5cqw`). Size all stage type and space in `cqi`/`cqh` against this frame. A 720p TV, a 1440x900 laptop (16:10) and an ultrawide then all get the same composition, with letterbox areas filled by the vignette background, not stretched content. This is what TV-first game UIs effectively do, and it ends the "different on every screen" problem.
- **Two token families, one ratio:** stage tokens `--stage-step-*` in `cqi` of the stage frame, and phone tokens `--step-*` in Utopia `clamp(rem + vi)` from 320 to 480 px wide. Both use the same ratio (a 1.25 major third for phone, 1.333 for stage) and the same step names, so a "step 3 heading" means the same role everywhere. Suggested stage roles, as fractions of stage width at 1920 px: prompt about 3.4cqi (about 65 px), answer card text 2.6 to 3.2cqi, names and labels 1.5cqi, scores 4 to 6cqi, callout stamp 8 to 10cqi. Treat these as starting points for tuning, not sourced numbers.
- **Prompt fitting:** cap prompt measure at about 28 to 34 characters per line on stage (huge type, read from across a room) with `max-inline-size: 22ch`-ish in Dogbangy, use `text-wrap: balance`, and step the size down by length bucket: ≤60 chars uses step 4, ≤120 step 3, beyond that step 2 in Inter. Data-driven length buckets (set a `data-len="s|m|l"` attribute from JS by character count) are more predictable than fit-to-width tricks for multi-line text. Reserve fit-to-width for single-line callouts and names.
- **Answer card fitting:** each card is its own `container-type: inline-size`. Text is `clamp(var(--min), Xcqi, var(--max))` and also length-bucketed. Cards in a row share the same font size, the size of the longest answer's bucket, so one card doesn't shout louder than its rival. This is important for fairness in a head-to-head.
- **Layout grids by answer count** (stage frame):
  - 2 answers (head-to-head): two equal columns, `grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr`, gap about 4cqi, cards about 40cqi wide, prompt above. A "VS" or neutral divider is optional.
  - 3 answers (Thriplash-like): three columns at 16:9; on narrow frames, a stacked list.
  - 4 answers: 2x2.
  - 5 to 8 answers: a uniform grid, `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(28cqi, 1fr))`. That gives 3 columns for 5 to 6 answers and 4 columns for 7 to 8 answers. Use uniform card heights per row, each card 1 to 3 lines max, and smaller type bucket. Never mix card sizes among voting options, so nothing looks favored.
- **Phones-only mode:** the "stage" becomes the top of each phone. Switch layouts with `@container stage (aspect-ratio < 1)` rather than viewport media queries, so the same component handles a portrait phone (stacked cards) and a TV (columns). Respect `env(safe-area-inset-*)` on phones.
- **Zoom/accessibility:** keep a rem term in phone clamps, and keep max/min ≤ 2.5. Stage text sized in pure `cqi` won't respond to browser zoom. That is acceptable for a TV display, but provide a host "text size" setting that multiplies a `--stage-scale` custom property. Jackbox offers no such setting as far as sources show, so this would be an improvement over Jackbox.
- **Fix for the current codebase:** replace the scattered one-off clamps (RoomBits, Podium, Stat, StageLobby, page.module.css) with the shared tokens above. Ad hoc `vw` middles with different slopes are the main source of inconsistent sizing at intermediate widths.

### Gaps
- No published Jackbox numbers for stage font sizes or safe areas. The Android/Fire TV/Roku figures are the best proxy. The 12sp/18sp Android TV minimum text sizes appeared only on an unofficial mirror and were not verified.
- No tested data on ideal characters-per-line for party-game prompts read at 10 ft. The 28 to 34 character figure is an inference.

## 4. Motion choreography for comedic reveals and performance-safe web animation

### Takeaway
Comedic reveals work as a TV-paced sequence: setup (prompt alone), present (answers in, anonymous), vote, then reveal one beat at a time (tally, attribution, points, winner flourish), with a hold after each beat so the laugh lands before the next element moves. Jackbox calls this "maintain pacing" and treats its games as TV-hybrid. On the web, animate only `transform` and `opacity`, avoid animated blur, shadows and filters, use `will-change` sparingly, and replace scale and pan with fades under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Offer a host-level motion-sensitivity toggle as Jackbox does.

### Cited Findings
- The Jack Principles' first rule is to maintain pacing: one task at a time, with the game reacting before moving on. Laban describes the games as borrowing TV "narrative timing, transitions, and dialogue". — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)
- A Tee K.O. 2 bug that played "EVERY SINGLE ANIMATION that was possible at the same time" is recalled as a comic disaster. Simultaneous motion destroys readability. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)
- The PP10 team spent significant time making a sync-up moment feel "almost like a ritual", so moments of anticipation are deliberately designed. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)
- Jackbox ships a motion-sensitivity toggle that "reduces motion in games with many moving parts" (Quiplash 3 and others). — [Jackbox: PP7 accessibility](https://www.jackboxgames.com/new-accessibility-features-in-the-jackbox-party-pack-7/)
- web.dev: "restrict animations to `opacity` and `transform` to keep animations on the compositing stage". It says to avoid properties that trigger layout or paint, and notes that animating `top`/`left` causes layout while `translate()` avoids it. Shadows and blurs are expensive to paint. Use `will-change` sparingly, "only if you encounter a performance issue", apply it just before the change and remove it after. — [web.dev: Animations guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` has been Baseline since January 2020. MDN names scaling or panning large objects as vestibular triggers and recommends swapping motion for gentler alternatives such as an opacity dissolve. — [MDN: prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)
- Fibbage's reveal order, with lies one by one (each with who was fooled) and the truth last, is a sourced example of a suspense ladder that ends on the biggest payoff. — [TechRaptor Fibbage review](https://techraptor.net/content/fibbage-review); [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/fibbage-review-your-next-hit-party-game/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended head-to-head (Grand Rounds) timeline**, as starting values to tune in playtests (not sourced durations):
  1. Prompt in: 400 ms ease-out rise and fade, hold 1.2 to 1.8 s (or read-aloud length) alone on screen.
  2. Answer A drops in (spring, 450 ms), then a 600 ms beat, then answer B drops in. Staggering lets the room read A and react before B lands. Hold until voting starts.
  3. Voting: cards stay still, and only a timer and voter-count chips move. No motion competes with reading.
  4. Reveal: cards "lock" (a tiny squash, 150 ms). Voter avatars pop under each card one by one, 120 to 180 ms stagger, alternating sides or by card. Then a 500 ms pause, then each card's author name flips or slides in (the attribution laugh), then hold 1.5 s.
  5. Points: the number counts up on the winning card with a scale pop at the end, hold 1 s.
  6. Unanimous winner: the loser card dims (opacity 0.45, slight translateY down), and a stamp callout (iatrome's equivalent of "Quiplash", for example a "Unanimous" or "Clean sweep" stamp in Dogbangy) slams in at 1.3 to 1 scale with a 6 to 10 degree rotation, with confetti and a sound cue. Hold 2 s before advancing.
  7. Tie or jinx: a distinct, deadpan treatment (both cards wobble and a flat stamp appears) rather than the celebration.
  The rule throughout is that nothing new starts moving until the previous beat has settled and been held for at least 400 to 600 ms.
- **Multi-answer reveal (3 to 8):** reveal from lowest to highest votes, or from losing lies to the truth as in Fibbage, so the room escalates toward the biggest moment. Each answer gets the same mini-sequence (spotlight, voters, author, points) at a quicker tempo, and the winner gets the long hold.
- **Spotlight technique without filters:** dim non-focused cards with opacity and a translucent scrim layer (opacity-animated), not `filter: blur()`/`brightness()` or `backdrop-filter`, which are paint-heavy, especially on TV browsers and cheap laptops. Raise the focused card with `transform: scale(1.04) translateY(-1%)`.
- **Gloss and shadow without animating them:** pre-render jelly gloss and drop shadows as static layers (pseudo-elements or SVG). Animate the transform or opacity of the layer that holds them, never `box-shadow` values. To make a shadow grow on hover or win, cross-fade the opacity of a second, larger shadow layer.
- **Count-up numbers:** update text in rAF at most at 30 Hz with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so width doesn't jitter.
- **Use the existing easing tokens:** `--ease-spring` for entrances and stamps, `--ease-out` for exits and dims. Add duration tokens (`--dur-quick 150ms`, `--dur-base 300ms`, `--dur-enter 450ms`, `--beat 600ms`, `--hold 1500ms`) and drive choreography from the server-authoritative phase clock, so stage and phones stay in sync. This mirrors the FixyText "internal clock" lesson.
- **Reduced motion:** honour both the OS `prefers-reduced-motion` and a host setting that sets `data-motion="reduced"` on the stage root. In reduced mode, swap slams, pops and slides for opacity cross-fades. Keep the beats and holds (pacing is comedy, not motion). Disable confetti or reduce it to a static burst.
- **Sound:** sound effects are a large part of the reveal "landing" (cue on the stamp, ticks on vote pops), but no source here documents Jackbox's audio choreography. See Gaps.

### Gaps
- No source gives Jackbox's actual reveal durations, stagger intervals, or sound-cue design. All timings above are suggested starting points for playtesting.
- I did not retrieve Material Design 3 motion duration tokens (the page did not render through the fetcher). A sourced duration scale was therefore not available. The suggested tokens are inferences.

## 5. Spacing systems and cohesion across stage and phone

### Takeaway
Cohesion comes from one shared system: the same modular type steps, a space scale derived from type step 0, the same radii, outline weight, shadow and gloss tokens, and the same easing and duration tokens. That system is expressed in container-relative units on the stage and in rem plus vi clamps on phones. Jackbox's own model is "shared structure and UI, art sets each game apart". So iatrome's jelly component system should be identical across modes, and each mode may vary only accent colour, background motif and callout copy.

### Cited Findings
- Jackbox's games "share structure and general UI" while "the art sets them apart". Artists meet regularly to keep the pack coherent. — [Jackbox blog: PP10 art](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- Warren Arnold: the director's job is "making sure all parts of the game work as a cohesive unit". — [Jackbox blog: PP10 directing](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)
- Utopia's space scale derives from type step 0 through multipliers (for example 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6). It also uses "space pairs" (for example `--space-s-l`) that interpolate between two steps for more dramatic fluid spacing. — [Smashing Magazine: Meet Utopia](https://smashingmagazine.com/2021/04/designing-developing-fluid-type-space-scales/); multiplier list inferred from [Utopia blog URL parameters](https://utopia.fyi/blog/clamp/) (`s=0.75|0.5|0.25,1.5|2|3|4|6`), so treat it as indicative.
- Jackbox's controller uses a small, fixed component vocabulary (draw, buttons, text), giving cross-game familiarity. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)

### Inferences
- **Token architecture for iatrome** (extending `apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css`):
  - `--step--1 … --step-5`: phone and app type, Utopia clamps for 320 to 1240 px, ratio 1.2 to 1.25, base 16 to 18 px.
  - `--stage-step-0 … --stage-step-6`: stage type in `cqi` of the stage frame, same ratio family, base about 1.5cqi.
  - `--space-3xs … --space-3xl` plus pairs (`--space-s-l`), derived from step 0 with Utopia multipliers. Stage equivalents are `--stage-space-*` in `cqi`.
  - Radii should scale with the space scale on stage (for example card radius = `--stage-space-s`). Fixed 12/20/28 px radii look tiny on a TV and huge on a phone.
  - Outline width tied to scale: `max(2px, 0.12cqi)` on stage, so the ink outline stays visually consistent.
  - Durations and easings as in section 4.
- **Component contract:** one `AnswerCard` component used by Grand Rounds, trivia and drawing captions, with props for `state: idle | spotlight | dimmed | winner | jinx`, `size: from count`, `letter`, `author?` and `votes?`. One `PromptBanner`. One `Stamp` callout. One `ScoreTicker`. Phones use `VoteButton` with the same letter chip and colour as its stage card. Each mode customises only accent, background motif and stamp copy, following the Jackbox model of shared UI with distinct art.
- **Spacing rhythm rule:** inside a card, use padding of one space step and a gap of half that step. Between cards, use two steps. Between the prompt and the card row, use three steps. The stage safe inset is fixed at 5%. Keep the same ratios on phone using phone tokens, so screenshots of both feel like one product.
- **Consistency audits:** lint CSS modules for raw `px`/`vw` literals in `font-size`, `gap`, `padding` and `margin` (stylelint `declaration-property-value-disallowed-list`), so new components must use tokens.
- **Contrast:** answer text on jelly surfaces must hit at least 4.5:1, which is general accessibility guidance surfaced in search. Information must never be colour-only. Pair letters, avatars or icons with palette colours, since the palette's sky/rose/gold differ mainly in hue.

### Gaps
- No Jackbox source describes their internal spacing or type tokens. The architecture above is a synthesis of the Utopia method and Jackbox's stated "shared UI" principle.
- Utopia's exact default space multipliers were inferred from URL parameters, not from documentation text.
